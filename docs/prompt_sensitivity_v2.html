Why this study existed

A selector is the LLM under test: it reads a survey question and names what it would want to know in order to predict the answer. A prompt pack is a locked pair of wordings — the system role plus respondent vs person in the user prompt. The confirmatory run needs that pair chosen before it starts.

The first prompt study only swapped the system line, never touched “respondent,” and had no same-prompt noise floor. v2 asks whether the current wording pulls models into survey-research space relative to an analyst+person bundle and to no system prompt — and whether that shows up as better predictions.

What we expected, if the boxing were real and useful: more asks about the questionnaire itself, a larger list-rewrite on everyday questions than on political ones, and a VoR gain. We switch only if an alternative both rewrites the lists and predicts better. Lists that change while VoR stays put are a methods paragraph.

What we actually ran

Country-named prompts only — the same grain as the oracle (the data-derived ranking of which other questions predict the target). Extractor (Qwen) and disambiguator (Nemotron) stay fixed. Temperature 0 for the lock jobs. Two extra draws of the default at temperature 1.0 (t1/t2) sit on the Jaccard chart as sampling noise; they are not the bar an alternative has to beat.

Do the named features change?

Soft Jaccard is the overlap of two extracted feature lists, allowing paraphrase. Embed each item twice (the label, and “label: context”). Pair items one-to-one (Hungarian matching) when the better of those two cosines is at least 0.75. If m pairs match:

Soft Jaccard = m / (|A| + |B| − m)

1 means the lists name the same things; 0 means nothing matched. Mapped survey codes use ordinary set overlap (|A ∩ B| / |A ∪ B|) — mentioned once below, not charted.

X: selector · Y: mean Soft Jaccard, 0–1 · Source: ps_v2_digest.md, 68 cells. A pack bar below the r1–r2 bar means the wording changed the list more than a second greedy draw.

MiniMax and Hermes sit clearly below their r1–r2 bar. DeepSeek and Kimi do not: two greedy draws already overlap only 0.27–0.29, and the pack bars are in the same place. Temperature 1.0 is noisier still (Hermes t1–t2 = 0.37 vs greedy 0.64), so a 0.02 dip is not a prompt finding. MiniMax and Hermes rewrite more on everyday questions than on political ones — the direction the boxing hypothesis predicted. Mapped codes tell the same story for those two models; DeepSeek’s codes actually overlap slightly more under the alternatives than under r1–r2 (labels reworded, variables not).

Almost every extracted item is a respondent attribute on every pack. The survey-methods tell (asks about wording, interviewer, mode) is rare. Kimi used it on 20 of 68 default cells and 6 of 68 analyst cells. That is the whole “un-boxing” of type mix, and those items are not mapped to questions, so they cannot help VoR.

Does prediction get better?

Value-over-random (VoR) is how much better a downstream model predicts with the selector’s features than with a random set of the same size. Spearman for ordinal targets, log-loss for binary/nominal; above zero means the selector beat random. r1 and r2 sit on the chart so replicate bounce is in the same units. These means are 62 cells nested in 21 questions — not 62 independent trials.

Six Asian cells have no VoR

Q174 in Indonesia, Philippines, and Taiwan; Q137 in Philippines and Taiwan; Q102 in Philippines. Spearman fields are NaN; captured importance and k still exist. For Hermes, list-rewrite on those six is larger than on the 62 that have VoR. The score comparison is the milder subset of the rewrite.

How many features were named

X: selector · Y: mean mapped codes per cell · Source: same, 68 cells. MiniMax names ~19 on the default and ~16–17 on the alternatives; Kimi ~17 vs ~15. VoR compares each list to a random list of that same length, so a shorter list can look worse without any single item being worse.

Decision

Applied as: keep the default unless an alternative both rewrites lists more than a greedy rerun and has VoR above both default draws (not a coin-flip of cell wins). That is the readable form of the pre-registered stop rule, not the original “sign of mean delta” clause.

Role and referent were bundled; we did not test which word did the MiniMax/Hermes rewrite. Stage 2 (crossing them) was gated on a VoR gain and does not run. Report the rewrite in methods. Do not treat prompt wording as an untested threat, and do not claim we searched the whole prompt space.

Still before the confirmatory sweep: draw the paper grid, v4 oracles for those cells, leakage screen, lock the selector zoo. This study only closes the prompt gate.
